How it works

From the alert in your inbox to a line in your ledger

No bank logins, no screen-scraping, no SMS permission. It reads the emails your bank sends anyway, and only those.

1 · Gmail, read-only

The email your bank already sends

A narrow search finds alerts from bank domains. OTPs, offers and double-sent alerts are screened out before anything is read.

2 · Claude, then a learned format

Read by AI, until it learns the layout

Claude pulls out the details. Once a bank's layout has been read the same way five times, the app reads it on its own and only spot-checks.

See it

From a fresh install to your spends, in under a minute

Recorded from the real app, against a made-up inbox. Nothing in it is anyone’s real data.

  1. Set it up in the browser. A setup code, your Claude key, your Gmail with an app password.
  2. Last month, already filed. The first sync reads your alerts back to the 1st of last month.
  3. New spends arrive on their own. Card and UPI alerts, read as they land.
  4. Spends, IOUs and loans in one ledger.
  5. Invite your household with a one-time link, and back it all up in one file.

What you get

One ledger for the whole picture

Built for Indian banks and UPI: rupees, lakhs and the alert formats they actually send.

Spends appear on their own

Card and UPI alerts from any bank, checked every 10 minutes. A new account opens on everything since the 1st of last month.

It asks instead of guessing

An Amazon charge with no item names gets a one-tap "Shopping or Electronics?", and your answer becomes a rule.

Splits and IOUs

One balance per person, whether it came from a split bill, a loan to a friend or a cab they paid for.

Loans that add up

What's still owed, EMIs left, the month you'll be debt-free, and what closing early would save.

Statements on demand

Import a bank statement PDF for the UPI spends alerts missed. Its password is used once and never stored.

Private on a shared phone

An optional PIN locks the app, and a tap masks every amount on screen.

Where your data lives

The server keeps almost nothing

You run the server. Your spends never sit in a database, not even yours: each person's are one file, on your computer or in their own Drive.

Your Gmail

Read-only with Google sign-in, or an app password

  • Searched for bank alerts and statements only
  • Can't send, change or delete email

Your computer, or your Drive

One file per person

  • Spends, rules, IOUs, loans and your PIN's hash
  • In Drive, the app can't see any other file

Your server

Per person: one small record

  • Google id, email, name and timestamps
  • The Google sign-in token or app password, encrypted with AES-256-GCM

Self-host

Start it anywhere, set it up in the browser

One command starts it with nothing to configure. The first screen asks for a setup code, your Claude key and your Gmail; then you invite your household with a link. No Google Cloud project needed.

# no questions: starts it and prints a setup code
$ ./setup.sh
# open http://localhost:8080 and follow the screen

Wherever you like

One container and one data folder: your laptop, a home server, a VPS. A laptop that sleeps catches up when it wakes.

Google Cloud, scripted

One script in Cloud Shell: a private bucket as the data folder, Cloud Run's free tier and an https:// address.

Move it any time

Back up everything in one passphrase-locked file; a new install restores it, saved access and all.

Under the hood

Engineering notes

A few decisions that shaped the code.

Learned formats, never generated code

Asking a model to write a parser per bank means running model-written code on text anyone can email you. Instead, formats are induced from Claude's verified answers by aligning two alerts from the same DKIM-verified sender, then have to agree with Claude five times before they read alone.

One account per request

Every signed-in request runs inside that account's AsyncLocalStorage context. The ledger store, the Google client and the write queues read the account from there, and throw if there isn't one.

Timezones that don't lose a morning

"Since the 1st of last month" is midnight in India, not UTC. Computing it in UTC quietly dropped every alert from the first five and a half hours of the month.

Fakes at the edges

End-to-end suites run the real server with only Google, Claude and Razorpay replaced. Any other network call fails the test. The same fakes power this site's live demo.
